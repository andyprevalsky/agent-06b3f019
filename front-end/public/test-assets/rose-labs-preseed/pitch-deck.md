## 2. The thesis
Intelligence is becoming abundant. Knowing how the real world responds is not.
A model can write a flawless plan to onboard ten thousand merchants. It cannot know which ones pick up the phone, which bank portal rejects the upload, or which message gets a signature back. That knowledge is in no training set, because it does not exist until someone does the work.
Rose does the work: we run thousands of agents on one real operational job and keep the record of what worked.

## 3. Proof: Rappi and enterprise pipeline
- Rappi pilot agreed (20 seats, starting soon; no signed contract yet): coordinating signatures from more than 100,000 restaurants in Mexico so Rappi can launch a product it is six months late on.
- 5 Fortune 500 companies in pipeline.
- Deliberately boring work: high volume, real-world, and nobody else wants it.

## 4. Why it's hard
Almost everyone in agents is making one agent reliable. One agent can't do a job with ten thousand pieces; a thousand agents can, if they don't collide.
- **Decomposition model** that cuts the job into parallel pieces.
- **Distributed runtime** that keeps many agents working on shared state, handling conflicts and partial failures, and reconciling results into one answer.
- **Memory:** every subtask's trace is stored with whether it worked.
A year of work on the part almost nobody else is building.

## 5. Why we win: a Waze for work
Every task charts a piece of the real economy: this merchant, this portal, this approval chain. The next task that crosses it starts from the map, whichever customer drew it.
- **Shared:** two delivery apps onboard the same restaurants; two fintechs fight the same bank portals.
- **Only made by use:** nobody can buy, scrape or synthesize it.
- **Perishable:** merchants and portals change, so a late entrant can't catch up by spending once.
The number that proves it: first-attempt completion on covered vs. uncovered territory.
